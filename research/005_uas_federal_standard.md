# 005: Human evidence in AI procurement

Revised November 7, 2026. This note supersedes the earlier CP.11 description and operator CTSS-delta thresholds. Those thresholds were not established human-capability measures.

## Why this matters

An AI system can satisfy a machine test while leaving its reviewer unable to understand, challenge, or reverse a recommendation. Procurement evidence should distinguish the system boundary from the person's ability and authority to use it.

## What to document

Identify the system/version, workflow, applicable requirements, human decision rights, test method, observed results, failed cases, and recovery. Keep threat severity, self-report, and demonstrated human performance separate. An improvement target is not an observed result.

The [UAS regulatory profile](https://github.com/CyberStrategyInstitute/ai-safe2-framework/tree/main/00-cross-pillar/unbiased-ai) references CSF concerns. It does not extend CP.1 through CP.10. See the [CSF crosswalk and its remaining method mismatch](../06-digital-ai-symbiosis/federal-procurement-alignment.md).

## Decision

Use the actual contract and owning standard to determine applicability. Agree task-specific acceptance evidence before testing. Do not prescribe a CTSS percentage change as proof that operators retained capability. This note establishes no government recognition, enforceability, or safe harbor.
