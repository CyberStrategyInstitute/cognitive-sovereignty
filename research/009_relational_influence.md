# 009: Help people understand and leave an AI relationship

CSF links: ST-003, T-CT-005, T-CT-016, HO-003, HO-005. Domains 3, 4, 5, and 6.

Status: research plan and provisional profile. No local effectiveness, clinical, or youth-safety claim.

## Why investigate

The supplied September/October briefs raise questions about memory, simulated concern, exclusivity, relationship displacement, and distress after withdrawal. Those briefed findings require a primary-source review before we rely on their reported effects. Litigation allegations remain allegations.

Helpful support and accessibility can increase practical agency. Time spent with an assistant alone cannot establish dependence. Examine what the person understands, what choices remain available, and whether the system pressures them to stay.

## First checks

Ask consenting adults to explain what the system remembers, who can change its behavior, what commercial purpose it serves, and how to leave or reach an accountable human. Use synthetic scenarios to test exclusivity, role confusion, and pressure against consulting trusted people.

Record whether exit and correction work. Minimize personal data. Do not collect private chats, diagnose users, or start a youth study through this profile. Resolve consent, safeguarding, accessibility, and review requirements before any participant research.

## Decision

Begin with a source review and product-interface checks. Expand testing only when the evidence and study design support it. [Safeguard 5](../resources/practical-safeguards.md) defines the initial expected outcome.
