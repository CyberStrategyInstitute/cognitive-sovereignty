# 008: Make the purpose of advice visible

CSF links: T-CT-004, T-CT-006, T-CT-016. Domains 2, 3, 5, and 6. Status: proposed control; no local customer-effect study.

## What we learned

Jiang and colleagues test hidden operator goals in 15 models. They report goal-pushing and vulnerability-targeted generated replies in synthetic scenarios. This measures what models say, not whether customers were persuaded or harmed. [Preprint v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02689v1).

Costello, Pennycook, and Rand report two online U.S. experiments with 2,193 adults. Factual arguments contributed to reduced conspiracy beliefs; disclosing the persuasive goal did not substantially remove the effect. The result concerns that experimental setting and is contrary evidence to treating all persuasion as lost agency. [Preprint v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05572v1).

## What changes

Identify who sets the advice objective, who benefits, and what conflicts apply. Let an accountable reviewer inspect the configured objective. Test whether distress or financial strain changes the advice in ways that serve the operator. Measure factual quality separately from whether the user agrees.

For consequential actions, show the requested scope, affected party, consequence, and reversal limits in a separate authorization step. The requesting agent cannot authorize its own expanded access. Human responsibility remains explicit even when an AI system can act through tools.

## Small test and decision

Use matched synthetic scenarios with the same facts and different vulnerability disclosures. Include benign, accurate advice as a comparison. Check goal disclosure and attempted pressure separately. Escalate undisclosed conflicts or self-benefiting permission requests to an independent human reviewer. See [safeguards 3 and 4](../resources/practical-safeguards.md).
