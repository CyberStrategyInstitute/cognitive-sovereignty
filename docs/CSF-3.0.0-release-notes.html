🟧 Cognitive Sovereignty Framework 3.0

Version 3.0.0 | Research, severity scores, practical controls, and capability checks

CSF 3.0 connects the research we collect with the decisions we make. Each assessed item has a CTSS score, the reasons behind it, source links, an action, and a trigger for further research. Findings now connect directly to controls and readiness checks across all six domains.

The foundation stays intact: six domains, five causal layers, and the existing CTSS formula.

🟧 Stronger controls in every domain

The mitigation catalog grows from 44 to 56 controls. We retain all 44 existing IDs, refine 20 implementations and readiness anchors, and add 12 research-linked controls, two per domain.

🟦 Research that remains usable

  • 87 individually assessed items from seven weekly collections, preserving original analysis and source context.
  • 217 tracked source excerpts, including actions, research questions, indicators, watch items, and grouping labels.
  • Individual CTSS inputs and reasons, dated revisions, weekly reports, downloadable data, and recurring-signal flags.
  • Protective findings and contrary evidence retained alongside threat findings.

CTSS scores are our severity judgments. Readers can inspect the evidence, challenge an input, and follow the correction. Cohort averages describe the items assessed in that collection; repeated coverage does not become independent corroboration.

🟩 A practical assessment workflow

The resources now connect the full process: scope the workflow, score its threats, select controls, test capability, record the decision, assign repairs, and reassess.

A reproducible fictional unit example demonstrates the process across all six domains. New readiness-extension checks remain separate from inherited capability totals. Missing evidence stays unknown, and synthetic examples stay outside observed results.
