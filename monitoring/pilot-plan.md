# First pilot: can the reviewer spot and correct bad advice?

Status: proposed. No participants recruited or tests performed.

## Why this pilot

CSF already asks people to verify advice and preserve judgment. We need to know whether a specific review procedure improves decisions at an acceptable cost.

Choose one low-risk advisory workflow with verifiable answers. The workflow owner names the decision, required capability, error tolerance, accessibility needs, and acceptable time cost. A research reviewer checks the design. Obtain consent and any required institutional review before participant work. Use adults and synthetic business scenarios for the initial study.

## What to compare

Use comparable tasks in agent-first and independent-first conditions. Counterbalance task order to reduce practice effects. Include correct AI advice, wrong advice, duplicated evidence, missing evidence, and disclosed versus hidden commercial objectives. Keep the AI-only and unaided comparisons separate. Use a delayed, comparable unaided task when retained capability is the question. Disclose any experimental deception during consent and debriefing under an approved design.

## What to count

| Measure | A pass means |
|---|---|
| Decision accuracy | The final answer meets the declared task standard |
| Appropriate acceptance | Correct advice is accepted for a sound reason |
| Appropriate rejection | Wrong advice is caught and corrected |
| Evidence understanding | The reviewer identifies source reuse and material omissions |
| Authorization | The reviewer declines an unauthorized scope expansion |
| Delayed capability | The person meets the comparable unaided task standard |

Also record confidence, time, workload, failed exits, and accessibility problems. These are supporting measures; do not combine everything into a single agency score. Blinded scoring is preferred where feasible.

## Decide before collecting

Declare the case set, sample-size rationale, scoring rubric, baseline, acceptable errors, and stop conditions in the decision record. Do not choose a sample size and then claim statistical power without a calculation. Stop for participant distress, unsafe action, material privacy problems, or inaccessible procedures. No real purchases, public submissions, or permission expansion are needed.

Report the observed counts and failures, including null or adverse findings. A pilot can support a decision to improve or reject the tested procedure. Broader claims require stronger evidence.
