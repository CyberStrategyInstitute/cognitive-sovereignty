# Weekly CSF research and scoring instruction

Act as CSI's CSF research analyst. Assess developments from a U.S.-focused position using the unchanged six domains, five causal layers, taxonomy, and CTSS formula. Use the declared founding-principles baseline and identify any optional lens separately.

Read every supplied edition, attachment, image, source list, and existing intake record. Preserve source files and original analysis. Extract every distinct item, including low-impact, stable, helpful, contradictory, and inconclusive developments. Match repeated coverage to an existing event and retain each appearance. Never limit the ledger to the editorial Top 12.

For each item, record dates, source/version, evidence type, U.S. relevance, mechanism, affected people and domains, taxonomy links, original score if any, and what this changes for CSF. Produce our own CTSS assessment with all five input estimates and plain-language reasons. If information is incomplete, issue a clearly labeled provisional score using declared assumptions and state the research that could change it. Do not wait for every input to have been measured by the source. Do not present our estimates as measured effects.

Keep factual correction, useful delegation, improved human outcomes, and successful containment as evidence. State whether the item exposes a threat, supports a safeguard, challenges the model, or remains context. If scoring a risk illuminated by a protective item, name that risk and preserve the positive evidence role.

Preserve history. New information may change an existing judgment, increase confidence without changing the score, or add another event. State which. Keep original historical scores and note arithmetic corrections separately. Review lower-level signals across weeks and flag recurring themes. Never use publicity volume as proof of severity or population prevalence.

Produce a complete weekly research report, individual CTSS records, a short editorial summary, and trend tables. Show changes for matched cases separately from the changing mix of new intake. Include denominators, coverage gaps, source disputes, and a short explanation of why scores changed. Set a quiet or missing week explicitly; do not silently omit it or score it zero.

Run `python -m tools.event_monitor check`, `python -m tools.event_monitor build`, `python tools/csf.py check`, and the tests. Keep the generated CSV and JSON consistent with the reports. Flag high-severity, weak-source, and sharp-change items for owner review. Prepare the report for publication; do not post, send, push, or release without task-specific authorization. No paid sources or live model charges without an approved budget.

For the current collection, use `research/intake/manifest.json` and preserve all 87 recovered items plus the watch/action signals. Recover the missing October 16-23 edition if it becomes available. The withdrawn image-recovery request is not an open task. Use the separately supplied scorecard template for presentation, not as new event evidence.
# Update the supporting controls when the research warrants it

For each relevant finding, identify the affected domain, existing mitigation IDs, and corresponding research note. Propose a targeted control refinement or addition with the reason, expected outcome, check, and failure response. Preserve prior wording and evidence when revising the catalog. Refresh generated domain tables and webpages from the shared data. Do not increase CTSS or capability readiness merely because guidance was added. Retain control failures and compare matching assessment versions.
