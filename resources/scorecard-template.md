# A scorecard people can inspect

Use the supplied six-panel layout as a presentation template. The first panel gives the overall assessment; one panel explains each factor. The [research hub](../docs/research.html#scorecard) applies this structure to the actual item ledger.

## Overall panel

Show the item, stable ID, event/publication dates if known, actual assessment date, scope, declared baseline/lens, provisional or reviewed status, CTSS, and priority band. List all five inputs and their point contributions. State the main consequence and the next research or escalation trigger. Keep facts, assumptions, and projected scenarios distinguishable.

## One panel per factor

Use the same order each time: what it measures, the 0-5 estimate, why that estimate fits this case, evidence and limits, point contribution, and a plain-language takeaway.

| Factor | Plain-language question | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Likelihood | How likely is the assessed condition within this scope and time horizon? | L × 5 |
| Impact on agency | What could it do to independent judgment, choice, authorship, participation, or self-direction? | Ia × 6 |
| Population reach | Who and how many could be affected in the declared scenario? | R × 4 |
| Detection difficulty | How hard is the mechanism or its use to notice and challenge? | D × 3 |
| Recovery difficulty | How hard is it to reverse the relevant technical, institutional, economic, or personal effects? | RecD × 2 |

These whole-point contributions are the existing weighted CTSS formula, unchanged. Unknown facts may support declared provisional estimates; an unfilled input is not silently zero. Keep a reader-friendly explanation above any technical detail.

## Visual standard

Use charcoal, warm white, CSI orange for structure and emphasis, and maroon for high-consequence decisions. Always show score, band, and status as text so color is not the only signal. Keep long labels and source links readable on small screens. The source PNGs are immutable references; accessible HTML uses text and live data.

## Historical example boundary

The attached Anthropic watermark case is dated September 3, 2026. Its inputs 5/4/5/4/3 calculate to 87. Preserve that historical example without implying that its deployment or harm claims were newly verified in this task. It is excluded from the 87-item weekly intake cohort. Its projected escalation to 95 does not supply changed inputs, so a new calculation must state those inputs before using that projection as a reproducible result.
